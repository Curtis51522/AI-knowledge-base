5. Discussion
The experimental results confirmed that positioning the tool center point (TCP) near
the middle of the robot’s working range leads to lower energy consumption than positions
closer to the base or at maximum reach. This finding, which was based on minimizing the
moment of inertia, was consistent across robots with similar constructions, suggesting that
placing the TCP at approximately 50% of the robot’s reach is the most energy-efficient.
Due to limited access to multiple ABB industrial robots, all measurements were conducted
using the simulation software, with initial verification against data from a physical robot.
An initial sample of seven robots with varying reach ranges from 0.5 to 4.2 m was
tested, revealing different optimal positions for minimum energy and time demands. Sig-
nificant design differences between the robots led to inconsistencies, making the sample
unsuitable for the methodology. A refined sample of ABB robots with similar designs and
range limits yielded more consistent results, which were suitable for further development.
The study highlights how structural characteristics affect energy consumption, par-
ticularly through the moment of inertia, which affects the robot’s resistance to rotation. In
articulated robotic arms, inertia is affected by the mass distribution in each segment and
its distance from the rotation axis. Robots with counterbalancing mechanisms required
less energy during vertical movements, whereas robots without such mechanisms showed
increased energy demands in extreme vertical positions. This shows that energy efficiency
is significantly affected by the design properties, such as counterbalancing mechanisms
and mass distribution, especially when frequent gravitational force compensation is re-
quired.

stabilityandtorquerequirements,especiallyforupwardmotions.

applications,thusprovidingvaluableinsightsforefficientandenergy-savingsolutionsin
